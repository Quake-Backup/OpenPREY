# openPR06 0.15.0: First Contact Edition

**openPR06 0.15.0 is the First Contact Edition, openPR06's first real release.** Play the original PREY (2006) on today's Windows, Linux and Mac computers with a modern open-source engine: OpenGL or Vulkan, shadow maps on every light, sun shadows across the Land of the Ancients, every loading screen recaptured at wide aspect, and a new interface for PREY's menus. Bring your own copy of PREY, play, and tell us what breaks.

## Highlights

- 🛸 **PREY on a modern engine.** openPR06 replaces PREY's engine with the open-source [openQ4](https://github.com/themuffinator/openQ4) engine, the same one behind openQ4 0.15.0, and its game code with one 64-bit module built from the PREY SDK for single-player and multiplayer. Your retail game files load as they are.
- 💻 **Packages for every platform openQ4 ships on:** Windows x64 and ARM64 (installer or zip), Linux x64 and ARM64 (`.tar.xz` or AppImage; ARM64 is a preview), and macOS on Apple silicon in OpenGL and Metal-bridge variants (preview).
- 💿 **Finds your copy of PREY.** CD-era and other retail installs are found through the registry, App Paths, uninstall entries and the usual install folders, with no Steam or GOG assumptions, and the game files are checked against their official checksums, CD layout included.
- 🌗 **Shadow maps on every light.** Shadows are now shadow-mapped by default on every preset that draws them, the sun casts cascaded shadows across the Land of the Ancients, and PREY's own skin, hair and organic-wall shaders take soft shadows like everything else.
- 🖼️ **Every loading screen, recaptured.** All 33 in-game loading screens were re-rendered at retail's own cameras with shadow-mapped lighting, and extended to fill widescreen and tall windows, so a level loads over its own art again.
- ✨ **PREY's effects, restored.** The glow overlay matches retail, forcefield and energy beams are visible again, mirrors and portals clip correctly, and heat haze holds up at any aspect.
- 🌍 **A 3D title screen.** The main menu opens on a hollow, lit Sphere and an Earth built from the game's own map, after a new start-up screen of portal energy.
- 🧭 **A new interface for PREY's menus.** The menus, loading screen and system check are now drawn as sharp, resolution-independent vectors at PREY's own layout, with full keyboard and controller navigation and a reorganised Options page with dozens of new settings.
- 🖥️ **Modern windows and displays** through SDL3: windowed, borderless and exclusive fullscreen, monitor selection, any aspect ratio, and native Wayland or X11 on Linux.
- 🤖 **Multiplayer bots (experimental):** nineteen named characters on PREY's own artificial player, each with a fighting style and chat. They walk PREY's walkways, take its portals and land their shots, and Create Server can fill a match with them.

## What openPR06 is

openPR06 replaces PREY's engine and game binaries with its own: a GPLv3 engine built on openQ4, and a source-available game module built from Human Head Studios' PREY SDK. It runs the official retail PREY (2006) assets and ships none of them. You need your own copy of PREY, from the original discs or another legitimate source.

This is the first release built on the openQ4 engine. The nightly builds from April 2026 on the Releases page predate that move and are superseded by this one.

## Getting started

- **Finds CD-era installs on its own.** openPR06 checks the folder it starts in, the Windows registry (install keys, App Paths and uninstall entries) and the usual install folders such as `Human Head Studios\Prey` and `2K Games\Prey`. On Linux it looks in `~/Games/Prey`. If it can't find yours, point it at the folder that contains `base`: see [If auto-detection fails](https://github.com/themuffinator/openPR06/blob/v0.15.0/docs/user/getting-started.md#if-auto-detection-fails).
- **Checks your game files.** Startup confirms the official base packs are present, and both the consolidated retail layout and the CD layout's `pak000`–`pak004` are checksum-verified.
- **Pick the package for your platform.** Windows: the `-setup.exe` installer or the `.zip`, for x64 or ARM64. Linux: the AppImage (`chmod +x` it first) or the `.tar.xz`, for x64 or the ARM64 preview. macOS: the `openPR06.app` archive for Apple silicon, OpenGL or Metal bridge; it is unsigned, so right-click it and choose Open the first time.
- **One game module for every mode.** A single `game_<arch>` module, built from the PREY SDK, runs the campaign and multiplayer.
- **Every retail map loads.** In a load audit of an unmodified 3-CD install patched to 1.4, all 37 maps (25 single-player, 12 multiplayer) loaded on both OpenGL and Vulkan, and the shadow-map fallbacks that audit found have since been fixed.
- **Saves and settings of their own.** openPR06 keeps its configuration, saves and logs in its own folder and leaves your PREY install untouched.

## Graphics

- **OpenGL or Vulkan.** OpenGL is the default and draws PREY's original interaction stages. Vulkan (`r_renderApi vulkan`) reaches gameplay, plays PREY's timed screen effects and spirit-walk stages, and draws its glow overlay, but is not yet at full parity with OpenGL.
- **Shadow maps by default.** Every preset that draws shadows now uses shadow maps (`r_useShadowMap 1`), Low, Performance and Balanced included, with stencil shadows as the per-light fallback.
- **Sun shadows in the Land of the Ancients.** The sun's shadow maps now store the same side of each wall as a stencil shadow, so it no longer shines through the canyon walls of lotaa and lotab, and Quality and Ultra give it cascaded shadows that keep their detail close up.
- **PREY's own shaders take shadow maps.** Surfaces drawn by PREY's per-light programs (skin, cloth, hair, parallax, masked surfaces and the organic walls) take soft shadow-mapped shadows on OpenGL instead of hard stencil edges.
- **The glow overlay matches retail.** Glow was blurred too strongly and drawn about four times too bright, so the multiplayer maps' wall-walk panels turned into thick white outlines and energy effects into opaque bursts. It now follows retail's 256×256 blur, and includes portal skies.
- **Beams are back.** Forcefield, laser and energy beams that turn to face you were drawn edge-on and vanished; they now draw retail PREY's ribbon.
- **Mirrors, portals and fog.** Mirror and portal views clip at their surface, alpha-tested surfaces (Tommy himself among them) appear in mirrors and portals again on OpenGL, PREY's game portals register as retail does, and fog lights no longer paint over ambient-only surfaces (the black fog in `dmshuttle1` had blacked out its Earth window).
- **Distortion that holds up.** Heat haze, refraction and membrane effects keep their shape at wide aspect, in supersampled levelshots and in each VR eye.
- **Screenshots larger than the window** (`screenshot 3840 2160`) match a one-pass render on OpenGL and Vulkan, HUD included.
- **Engine features from openQ4:** graphics presets with Auto-Detect and a system check that offers the right preset on first start, optional character self-shadows, HBAO and ground-truth ambient occlusion beside SSAO, froxel volumetric haze, PNG, BC4/BC5, BC6H and BC7 texture replacement packs, and PNG screenshots (`r_screenshotFormat 1`). These come from openQ4 and are still being checked against PREY.

## Loading screens

- **Every level loads over its own art again**, as in retail PREY. Single-player levels had shown the generic painting and multiplayer levels their menu thumbnail.
- **All 33 loading screens are recaptured** in a new client-side pack, `pak2.pk4`: re-rendered at the retail cameras with shadow maps for every light, recovered from the retail art where needed, and extended with the tiles that fill wide and tall windows. A picture without those tiles is drawn at 4:3 rather than stretched.
- **The level's name hangs from the top of the screen** in a notch built from PREY's own interface furniture, which lowers into view and parts to fit the name as each load begins.

## The menus

- **A 3D title backdrop**, on by default: a hollow, lit Sphere and an Earth built from the game's own map, with an entrance as the menu opens (`ui_menuScene 0` keeps the stock painting).
- **A new start-up screen:** a ball of portal energy sends a beam up into a lens flare carrying the PREY wordmark while the engine loads.
- **Widescreen menus.** The stock menus' backdrop and loading screens fill wide and tall windows instead of sitting in a 4:3 box.

### A new interface

openPR06 rebuilds PREY's menus as a retained, vector interface that stays sharp at any resolution, at the stock screens' own layout and with their art. It is on by default; `ui_retained 0` in the console brings back PREY's stock menus.

- **The whole front end:** the title screen's arc of hooks, New Game, Load and Save Game, Mods, Credits, the death screen, Options, the system check, the loading screen, MultiPrey, the connecting screen, message boxes and the multiplayer chat line.
- **Keyboard and controller navigation everywhere**, with a visible focus and whole rows as the pointer's target, PREY's own arrow as the pointer, and quiet hover and step sounds from PREY's console (`ui_retainedCueSounds 0` turns them off).
- **Pages open and close smoothly:** Options' frame costs about 1.5 ms where it dropped frames at 60 Hz, and MultiPrey, Load Game, Save Game and Credits open in under a millisecond.
- **A reorganised Options page:** two columns of compact rows under section headings, new Mouse and Controller tabs (acceleration, dead zones, look curves, gyro, rumble, button prompts), HUD, Accessibility and System tabs (field of view, weapon field of view, reduced motion, text backing, FPS counter), and new Video and Audio rows (upscaling, shadow map size and filtering, temporal AA, motion blur, dialogue volume, occlusion). Bound keys are drawn as keycap, mouse and controller glyphs, and controllers have default bindings.
- **Talon UI Condensed**, a body face drawn for openPR06, sets every row, button and dialog. It keeps the squared bowls of the PREY wordmark with more open counters, so small text stays legible. It is licensed under the SIL Open Font License 1.1.

## Multiplayer

- **Multiplayer runs on the unified module.** A listen server with two clients has been played; wider testing is what this release asks for. See [Multiplayer Networking](https://github.com/themuffinator/openPR06/blob/v0.15.0/docs/user/multiplayer-networking.md).
- **openQ4's netcode and hardening**, network protocol 2.42: direct IPv4, IPv6 and hostname connections, LAN discovery over IPv6, and servers that resist forged packets and client floods.
- **Bots (experimental).** Nineteen named bots on PREY's own artificial player, each with one of six fighting styles, a retail player model and its own chat, added with `addbot [character] [skill]`. PREY's maps ship no bot navigation, so bots fight what they can see rather than plan routes. See the [bot guide](https://github.com/themuffinator/openPR06/blob/v0.15.0/docs/dev/mp-bots.md).
- **Bots that play PREY's arenas.** They walk onto wall-walk surfaces, hold on walls, ceilings and inside the spheres, refuse steps into voids and death volumes, and take the game portals they see. They aim at the body, lead each weapon by its real projectile speed and track a target's movement, and each skill level beats the one below.
- **Create Server starts a server with bots:** a new Bots tab sets how many, their skill, skill spread, chat and named characters. `g_artificialPlayerCount` now works on any server, not only with `developer 1`.
- **The server browser speaks PREY.** Its status line, scan progress and server details are labelled in English, French, Italian and Spanish, and a passworded server reads "PASSWORD REQUIRED" as in retail. Its Game type filter finds PREY's Deathmatch and Team DM servers again, a LAN server shows once instead of up to six times, and Create Server adds Team Balance and dims options that don't apply.
- **Fixes:** Jen and the Abducted can be hit on a dedicated server (shots passed through them), bots show a ping of 0, the standings box sits in the top-right corner on wide screens, and a match won at the frag limit no longer logs a message overflow.

## Menus, sound and fixes

- **PREY's stock menus behave as retail again:** tabs play their flip sound and start their scans (player models, maps, servers, control profiles), opening MultiPrey refreshes the server list, and Apply Changes restarts the video mode.
- **World screens work as retail:** consoles that an AI uses trigger their targets and play their sounds, the roadhouse games reset, and the jukebox turns on and off.
- **The pointer is retail size** on the menus and on in-world screens.
- **The consoles wear PREY's palette:** portal cyan on black in game, Sphere-shell slate on the system console.
- **The mix follows retail PREY's loudness.** The score takes the music volume and every other sound the master volume, both at retail's 0 dB, Quake 4's speaker attenuation (which put every positioned sound 3.7 to 6 dB under the score) is gone, and dialogue ducks the rest of the mix by 4.5 dB again, so the Roadhouse jukebox dips under Tommy's lines.
- **Controller buttons in the game's tips:** the campaign's tutorial plates ("C to crouch"), the shuttle and rifle-scope tips and the multiplayer ready and vote tips show the button of the controller in use, in Xbox, PlayStation, Nintendo, Steam Deck or generic labels. The Controls page keeps up to three keys and three buttons per action.
- **Damaged retail Ogg files play through.** Sounds that stopped early are decoded again with libvorbisfile, which resumes after a bad page.
- **The rifle scope's lens covers the whole screen** at any aspect.
- **The startup splash shows on Windows**, and light grids bake from the right view.
- **Updates:** the menu's Updates check asks openPR06's GitHub releases instead of PREY's retired master server. It reports what it finds and links the releases page; it never installs anything.

## Upgrade Notes

- **Install the complete 0.15.0 package** into its own folder. Keep the executables and `basepr/` together and don't mix files from different builds.
- **Leave your PREY install where it is.** Don't copy retail `.pk4` files into `basepr/`; that folder holds openPR06's own packs.
- **From the April nightlies or openPREY:** the project was called openPREY. The first run moves an `openPREY` save folder to the new `openPR06` name, and `openPREYConfig.cfg` is still read; `OPENPREY_*` environment variables are not.
- **Saves** restore fully in the openPR06 build and platform that wrote them. A save from another openPR06 build starts its level afresh, and retail PREY saves can't be restored, so finish a level before you upgrade.
- **Multiplayer needs openPR06 0.15.0 on every client and server.** Retail PREY and its servers can't join openPR06, and mods built for the original PREY game DLLs don't load.
- **Defaults changed once:** existing configurations are moved to shadow maps and TrueType text (`r_preyDefaultsMigrated`) and to the new interface (`ui_retainedDefaultMigrated`) one time. Set `ui_retained 0` afterwards to keep PREY's stock menus.

## Known Limitations

- **Full campaign completion and multiplayer parity aren't claimed yet.** Representative campaign maps (Roadhouse, the Feeding Tower, the deathwalk, the Shuttle, portal gameplay, the Land of the Ancients) and save/load have been played; please report anything that breaks a playthrough.
- **Vulkan** reaches gameplay but is not at full parity with OpenGL, which stays the default.
- **The new interface** covers the menus and loading screen; the HUD is still PREY's own. If a screen misbehaves, `ui_retained 0` returns to PREY's stock menus, and please report it.
- **Bots** don't plan routes, spirit walk or board shuttles, because PREY's maps ship no bot navigation.
- **Multi-view demos and voice chat** are unavailable: they need game calls PREY's game module doesn't have.
- **Platforms:** only Windows x64 has been play-tested. The Windows ARM64, Linux ARM64 and macOS packages are built, staged and checked in CI, which has no copy of PREY to run them with; Linux ARM64 and macOS are previews, and the macOS packages are unsigned and unnotarized. There is no Android package.
- Features that arrived with the openQ4 engine (temporal AA, dynamic resolution, cel shading, texture packs, the level-load cache, VR and controller profiles) are carried over and still being checked against PREY. Their guides say so.
